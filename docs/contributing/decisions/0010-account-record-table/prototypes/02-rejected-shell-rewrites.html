Direction I · console

Two columns that scroll independently

The account rail becomes a horizontal section bar. Title and filters share one toolbar row, so the split starts about 120 px down instead of 380 px. List rows are hairline-divided instead of carded, and each column keeps its own scroll — the list stays put while you read.

Direction II · table

A table, with the record docked beside it

Six uniform fields per package is a table, not a stack of cards. Name, id, app, tags and updated become sortable columns you can compare down a page. The record docks at the right instead of replacing the view, so picking through ten packages never loses your place.

Where I'd go: I as the shell, II inside it

They aren't rivals. Direction I is a shell — uncapped width, section bar, toolbar row, two independent scrollers — and every one of the 14 list/detail pages can adopt it unchanged. Direction II is what one page puts in the left column when its records are uniform enough to tabulate. Packages, jobs, tokens and admin users are; memories and secrets probably aren't.

  1. Build the shell. A new ConsoleShell beside AccountManagementShell — uncapped, section bar in the header, toolbar row, two scroll columns. Migrate Packages onto it first, alone, and live with it for a day.
  2. Move the record content over. Emergent metadata columns, ids as copy chips, search index boxed behind a disclosure. This is the part that also fixes phones.
  3. Then tabulate, page by page. Only where the records are uniform, and only after the shell has settled.

Direction III is the right answer for one case I'd flag separately: a package deep-linked from outside the account area. Worth having as a route eventually, not worth having instead of a list.
